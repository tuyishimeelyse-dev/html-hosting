Why Blanket Intercropping Recommendations Fail Across Rwanda

Across multi-cycle field experiments in Rwanda, cumulative 12-month net profit in cassava-cereal-legume systems spans an extraordinary range—from a net financial loss of -$412/ha (Cycle 2 annual Maize–Beans rotation in Kayonza) to $14,175/ha (Cycle 2 Low Maize / High Cassava Treatment E in Karongi). Deploying a single, uniform intercropping package across Rwandan districts inevitably causes severe economic underperformance in at least half of target geographies.

To equip agricultural extension programs (such as One Acre Fund field teams) with objective, field-measurable targeting rules, we trained a Block-Grouped 5-Fold Cross-Validated Random Forest model (R² = 0.73, n = 159 plots) alongside CART Regression Decision Trees combining multi-window rainfall metrics (1–24 MAP), dry-spell indices, spatial row geometry (arrangement and interrow_cm), fertilization, planting dates, and Season B bean root rot severity (beans_rootrot_inc_r1).

The Three-Tier Extension Targeting Hierarchy:
  1. Tier 1 — Hydrological Regime (rain_mm_1_3MAP = 357 mm): Determines whether soil moisture during Months 1–3 supports simultaneous cereal establishment and cassava tuber initiation (Humid ≥ 357 mm) or induces below-ground competition (Semi-Arid < 357 mm).
  2. Tier 2 — Spatial Row Geometry (arrangement & interrow_cm): Within humid zones, shifting from 3-bean-row wide alleys (C-B-B-B-C) to 2-bean-row high-density cassava (C-B-B-C, Treatment E) at interrow_cm ≥ 55 cm (60 cm) boosts profits by +$3,500 to +$4,500/ha. Within semi-arid zones, avoiding cover crops and using single-row Akilimo (C-B-C-B-C) under drought prevents crop failure.
  3. Tier 3 — Biotic & Residue Management (beans_rootrot_inc_r1 < 2 & stover ≥ 5 t/ha): In Karongi, keeping Season B bean root rot incidence below score 2 protects +$3,248/ha in system profit ($12,000 vs. $8,752/ha).

2. Multi-Window Rainfall Dynamics (1–24 MAP Seasonal Regimes)

Cumulative rainfall (mm) by 3-Month Period After Planting (MAP) across all 3 experimental cohorts.

Figure 2.1: Cumulative Rainfall (mm) by 3-Month Period After Planting — Trial 1 Cycle 1 (25AB) Red Dashed Line = 357 mm Global Tree Threshold

1. The 357 mm Early-Season Establishment Threshold (1–3 MAP)

Across all three cohorts, 100% of Karongi plantings exceeded 357 mm during the critical 1–3 MAP window (402 mm in T1 Cycle 1, 444 mm in T1 Cycle 2, and 362 mm in T2 HarvestTime), whereas 100% of Kayonza plantings fell below 357 mm (335 mm, 264 mm, and 282 mm). Because cassava initiates storage roots between 30 and 90 days after planting, rainfall ≥357 mm allows cassava and intercropped maize to coexist without water stress, whereas <357 mm triggers competitive root-zone moisture depletion by maize.

2. Late-Cycle Starch Bulking Deficit (10–12 MAP) in Kayonza

During Months 10–12 after planting—when maize and beans have been harvested and cassava undergoes rapid starch accumulation—Kayonza suffered severe terminal dry spells across all cohorts: 150 mm (T1 Cycle 1), 53 mm (T1 Cycle 2), and just 35 mm (T2 HarvestTime), compared to 268 mm, 109 mm, and 98 mm in Karongi. Furthermore, Year 1 total precipitation in Karongi (1,283–1,367 mm) consistently exceeded Kayonza (860–1,020 mm) by +263 to +438 mm/year.
